If you build open source, developer tools or anything people rely on, at some point you'll want a way for users to say thanks with money. Buy Me a Coffee is the best-known tip jar, but it isn't the only option, and for developers it often isn't the best fit.

Here's how the main options compare on fees, where the money goes and who each one suits. Fees are as published at the time of writing; check each platform's pricing page before you decide, because they change.

## The quick comparison

| Platform | Platform fee | Money goes to | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GitHub Sponsors | 0% from personal accounts; up to 6% from organizations | Your GitHub Sponsors payouts | Recurring support from developers already on GitHub |
| Ko-fi | 0% on one-off tips (free plan); 5% on memberships and shop | Your PayPal or Stripe | Simple one-off tips |
| buymeatokens | 0% | Directly to your own Stripe, PayPal or USDC wallet | Tips framed around the AI tools you pay for |
| Buy Me a Coffee | 5% | Your payout account via Stripe | Creators who want tips, memberships and a shop in one place |
| Patreon | 10% for pages launched since August 2025 | Patreon payouts | Membership communities with regular perks |
| Open Source Collective | 10% host fee | Your collective's balance, spent transparently | Projects that need fiscal hosting and shared budgets |

Payment processing fees from Stripe, PayPal or card networks apply on top of all of these, and payout or currency conversion fees can apply too.

## GitHub Sponsors

[GitHub Sponsors](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/sponsors/sponsoring-open-source-contributors/about-sponsorships-fees-and-taxes) is the natural first stop for open source. Sponsorships from personal accounts carry no GitHub fee. Sponsorships from organizations can carry up to 6%: a card processing part and a GitHub service part.

- **Pros:** zero fee for individual sponsors, a Sponsor button on your repo, monthly tiers, and sponsors who are already logged in.
- **Cons:** you need to be accepted into the program in a supported region; supporters need a GitHub account; it's built around monthly sponsorship, which is a bigger ask than a one-off thank-you.

## Ko-fi

[Ko-fi](https://help.ko-fi.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002506494-Does-Ko-fi-take-a-fee) takes no platform fee on one-off donations on its free plan, and a 5% fee on memberships, shop items and commissions unless you pay for its Gold plan.

- **Pros:** free for simple tips, very low friction for supporters, payments to your own PayPal or Stripe.
- **Cons:** its design and audience lean toward artists and creators, so it can feel out of place in a developer README.

## Buy Me a Coffee

[Buy Me a Coffee](https://help.buymeacoffee.com/en/articles/8105744) charges a 5% platform fee on tips, memberships and product sales, plus processing fees.

- **Pros:** polished pages, memberships, digital products and a recognizable brand.
- **Cons:** 5% of everything, and "a coffee" doesn't connect to what developers actually spend money on.

## Patreon

Patreon is built for ongoing memberships with perks: posts, early access, Discord roles. It takes a 10% platform fee on pages launched since August 2025, with older pages on legacy rates.

- **Pros:** strong membership tooling and a community feel.
- **Cons:** the highest fee here, and it expects you to produce member content. Most maintainers just want to be thanked for the code.

## Open Source Collective

Open Collective with [Open Source Collective](https://opencollective.com/opensource/updates/fees-arent-changing-but-how-we-show-them-is) as fiscal host gives a project a transparent shared budget: anyone can see what came in and what was spent. The host charges a 10% fee.

- **Pros:** fiscal hosting (you don't need your own legal entity), transparent spending, good for projects with several maintainers and company sponsors.
- **Cons:** 10% fee and more process; overkill for one person who wants tips.

## buymeatokens

[buymeatokens](/) is a support page built around a specific idea: developers spend real money on AI tools, so supporters buy them AI token bundles (1M, 3M or 5M) at a price the creator sets.

- **Pros:** 0% platform commission; money goes straight to your own Stripe, PayPal or USDC wallet, so nobody holds your funds; a README badge with a live count of tokens bought; a concrete, honest ask ("help cover my AI tokens").
- **Cons:** it's young and in early access. You connect your own payment account, there's no recurring support yet (it's on the [roadmap](/roadmap)), and USDC support is experimental. Supporters are buying a thank-you measured in tokens, not provider credits.

Pages created during the current testing period get lifetime free access to creator features; details are on the [about page](/about).

## How to choose

- **Most of your supporters are developers who'd sponsor monthly:** GitHub Sponsors.
- **You want one-off thank-yous with the least friction:** Ko-fi or buymeatokens, both with no platform fee on one-off support.
- **Your costs are mostly AI tools and you want the ask to say so:** buymeatokens. See [What AI costs an open source maintainer per month](/blog/ai-costs-open-source-maintainers).
- **You're building a membership community with perks:** Patreon or Buy Me a Coffee memberships.
- **A multi-maintainer project needs a shared, transparent budget:** Open Collective.

## You don't have to pick just one

GitHub's `FUNDING.yml` shows several funding links on your repo, and a README can carry more than one badge. A common setup is GitHub Sponsors for recurring support plus a one-off option for people who want to say thanks once. Our guide on [adding a support button to your GitHub README](/blog/add-support-button-github-readme) shows how to set up both.

Whatever you choose, the placement and wording matter more than the platform. [How to get your users to cover your AI bill](/blog/fund-your-ai-tokens) covers where to ask and what to say.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the cheapest Buy Me a Coffee alternative?

For one-off tips, Ko-fi's free plan and buymeatokens both take 0% platform fee, and GitHub Sponsors takes no fee on sponsorships from personal accounts. Payment processing fees still apply everywhere.

### Is GitHub Sponsors better than Buy Me a Coffee for open source?

For recurring support from developers, usually yes: personal-account sponsorships carry no platform fee and the button sits on your repo. Buy Me a Coffee is better if you want memberships and products in one place.

### Does buymeatokens hold my money?

No. Payments go directly to your own Stripe, PayPal or USDC wallet. buymeatokens never holds funds and takes 0% platform commission.

## The short version

- Fees range from 0% (GitHub Sponsors from personal accounts, Ko-fi tips, buymeatokens) to about 10% (Patreon, Open Source Collective), plus processing.
- Match the platform to the kind of support: recurring, one-off, membership or fiscal hosting.
- Combining a recurring option with a one-off option covers most supporters.
